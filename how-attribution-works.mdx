Attribution is the whole point of seenpaid, so it's worth understanding what happens under the hood. It's four steps, and it's all automatic once you're set up.

## The chain

<Steps>
  <Step title="A tracked link is minted">
    When you publish, seenpaid replaces your product URL with a short tracked link on `go.seenpaid.com`. Each link is unique to that post, so a click can always be traced back to its source.
  </Step>
  <Step title="A click is recorded">
    Someone taps the link. seenpaid logs the click against the post and drops a first-party identifier so the visitor can be recognized through to checkout — no third-party cookies.
  </Step>
  <Step title="A Stripe sale fires">
    The visitor buys. Stripe sends seenpaid a webhook for the completed payment (checkout, payment link, direct charge, or subscription).
  </Step>
  <Step title="The sale is matched to the post">
    seenpaid matches the sale to the click, and the click to the post. The revenue now belongs to that specific post — visible in your dashboard in real dollars.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What counts as revenue

seenpaid attributes real Stripe events, including:

Refunds and chargebacks are subtracted back out, so your numbers stay honest over time — a post's earnings reflect money you actually kept.

## What you see

Once sales start matching, your dashboard surfaces:

- **Earnings per post** — the dollar figure attributed to each post.
- **Top earners** — your best-performing posts, ranked by revenue.
- **Revenue by platform** — which network actually drives sales, not just engagement.

<Note>
  Attribution needs two things live: at least one **published tracked post**, and a **connected Stripe account**. Until a post has been published, there are no tracked links to attribute against.
</Note>

## Honest by design

seenpaid never invents numbers. A post shows revenue only when a real Stripe sale matches a real click on its tracked link. If a sale can't be traced to a post, it isn't attributed to one — no fabricated credit, no inflated dashboards.
