Attribution is the whole point of seenpaid, so it's worth understanding what actually happens. The short version: **one identifier rides along from the click to the sale**, and matching that id at both ends is what ties a dollar back to a post. You don't wire any of it by hand — but knowing the chain makes the dashboard make sense, especially when a number looks off.

## The one-identifier idea

Every social platform shows you likes, reach, and clicks. None of them can tell you which post produced a paying customer, because the **post** lives on one surface (X, LinkedIn, TikTok…) and the **sale** happens on another (Stripe). The two never talk to each other, so the link between "this post" and "this dollar" is normally lost.

seenpaid recovers it by making one id survive the whole journey:

```
post → tracked link → click → your site → Stripe checkout → payment
         (cp_tid)                     (client_reference_id)
```

That id is called **`cp_tid`** (the seenpaid tracking id). Here's each hop.

## The chain, step by step

<Steps>
  <Step title="A tracked link is minted">
    When you publish, seenpaid replaces your product URL with a short link on `go.seenpaid.com/r/<id>`. The `<id>` is unique to that post, so any future click can be traced back to its source. On Instagram and TikTok — where caption links aren't clickable — the tracked link goes on your [bio page](/guides/bio-page) instead.
  </Step>
  <Step title="A click is recorded, and cp_tid is planted">
    Someone taps the link. The `go.seenpaid.com/r/<id>` redirect logs the click against the post, then forwards the visitor to your real destination — appending the tracking id as a `cp_tid` parameter and dropping a first-party cookie of the same name. No third-party cookies are involved.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The id carries into checkout">
    On your site, the [pixel](/guides/track-your-sales) reads `cp_tid` (from the URL or the cookie) and attaches it to your Stripe checkout as **`client_reference_id`** — the one field seenpaid's matching engine reads. For a bio-page Payment Link, the id is carried through directly, no pixel needed.
  </Step>
  <Step title="A Stripe sale fires">
    The visitor buys. Stripe sends seenpaid a webhook for the completed payment — Checkout Session, Payment Link, direct charge, or subscription invoice.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The sale is matched to the post">
    seenpaid reads `client_reference_id`, finds the tracked link it belongs to, finds that link's most recent click, and finds the post behind it. The revenue now belongs to that specific post, in real dollars.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## The reporting window

The revenue views summarize a rolling window — **7, 30, or 90 days** (30 is the default). A totals or breakdown figure counts only matched payments and clicks that fall inside the window you're looking at, and the "vs previous period" change compares it against the equal-length window just before it. Switching windows never changes history; it just changes the lens.

## Why some rows say "Direct" or "Unknown"

When seenpaid breaks your traffic down by source, not every click carries every detail. Rather than drop those clicks, it labels them honestly:

- **Direct** — a click with no referrer. The visitor typed the URL, came from an app that hides the referrer, or their browser stripped it. It's still a real, attributed click; seenpaid just doesn't know the page it came from.
- **Unknown** — a click seenpaid couldn't resolve for that dimension: no geolocation for the IP, or an unrecognized device, browser, or OS.
- **None** — used for UTM breakdowns when the link carried no UTM tag for that field (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, or `utm_campaign`).

These labels are grouping buckets, not errors. A large "Direct" share is normal for social traffic.

## Honest by design

seenpaid never invents a match. If a payment arrives with **no tracking id**, or one that doesn't resolve to a click, it is recorded as revenue but left **unattributed** — it is not credited to a random post. That's a deliberate choice: a fabricated match is worse than an honest "we don't know." The [attribution health](/guides/revenue-and-top-earners) figure shows exactly what share of your Stripe revenue was traceable to a post, so gaps are always visible rather than hidden.

## Privacy

- **First-party only.** The `cp_tid` cookie is set on your own destination as a first-party cookie; seenpaid uses **no third-party cookies** and no cross-site tracking network.
- **The pixel touches nothing on a direct visit.** If a visitor didn't arrive from a tracked link, there's no id to read and the pixel leaves the page completely alone.
- **The matching field is just an id.** `client_reference_id` is an opaque tracking id, not personal data. seenpaid matches on that id — it does not guess a buyer from their email.
