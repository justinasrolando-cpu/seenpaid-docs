---

Most "A/B testing" on social measures likes or clicks. **Experiments** in seenpaid measure **money**. You give one idea; seenpaid writes a few caption variants; each goes out as a real post with its own tracked link; and the winner is the one that *earned the most* — not the one that got the most engagement.

## When to use it

- You have a launch or a big post and want to find the hook that actually converts.
- You keep debating caption wording — settle it with revenue instead of opinion.
- You want to build a library of proven hooks to reuse and scale.

## How it works

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create the test">
    Give an **idea** (what the post is about), a **platform**, and how many **variants** you want (2–4, default 3). seenpaid drafts the variants. Nothing is live yet.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Launch it">
    Publish every variant. Each goes out as a real post carrying its **own distinct tracked link**, so clicks and sales land on the right variant independently.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Watch revenue decide">
    As sales come in, seenpaid ranks the variants by attributed revenue. The dashboard shows each variant with its clicks, sales, and dollars.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Reuse the winner">
    Take the winning caption and reuse or scale it — repost it, recycle it, or use it as the template for the next batch.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Info>
  Because each variant has its own tracked link, attribution is clean: a sale can only belong to the variant whose link the buyer clicked. That's what makes "winner by revenue" trustworthy rather than a guess.
</Info>

## Why revenue, not likes

A caption can rack up likes and drive nothing, or look plain and quietly convert. Optimizing for engagement optimizes for the wrong thing. Experiments rank on the number that actually matters — dollars earned — so scaling the winner scales revenue, not vanity.
