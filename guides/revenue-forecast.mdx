title: "Revenue Forecast"
description: "Predict what a draft would earn — a low–high range and the factors driving it — before you schedule it."
---

**Revenue Forecast** predicts what a draft post would earn *before* you publish it, based on your account's own history. It returns a dollar range and the specific factors pushing the estimate up or down — so you can compare two versions of a caption and pick the one likely to pay.

## When to use it

- You have two captions and can't decide which to send.
- You're wondering whether to add a link or media to a post.
- You want a gut-check on a draft before committing it to the calendar.
- You're building an approval flow: forecast a draft, and only schedule it if it beats your average.

## What you get

For a draft caption (plus optional platforms and whether it has media), Forecast returns:

- **A predicted range** — a low and high dollar figure, not a single false-precise number.
- **A score** — where this prediction lands between $0 and your best-earning post ever, as a rough 5–99 confidence-style number.
- **The factors** — up to four plain-language reasons the estimate moved, each marked up or down.

## How to read the range

The range is deliberately wide — the low end is roughly half the central estimate and the high end well above it — because a single post's earnings are genuinely uncertain. Use it for **comparison**, not as a promise: if caption A forecasts higher than caption B on the same account, that ranking is the useful signal.

<Info>
  Like [Money DNA](/guides/money-dna), Forecast needs at least **3 earning posts** before it will predict a number. Below that it tells you how far along you are (e.g. "2 of 3 so far") rather than faking a figure — a forecast with no baseline would be a guess dressed up as data, and that's against the house rule.
</Info>

## Using it in practice

<Steps>
  <Step title="Draft two versions">
    Write caption A and caption B for the same idea.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Forecast each">
    Run both through Forecast with the same platforms and media setting.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Ship the winner">
    Schedule the higher-forecast version, and read the factors to learn *why* it won — then apply that to the next post.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  This is a natural agent move: *"Forecast this caption, then schedule it if it beats my average."* The agent calls [`forecast_post`](/agents/tools-reference#forecast_post), reads the range, and only then calls `schedule_post`.
</Tip>
